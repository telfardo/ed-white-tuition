Academic info

Physics, maths & philosophy

Ed studied Physics and Philosophy at Bristol University and has tutored for five years. He has a highly visual way of presenting material — and you'll feel like equals in the lesson.

Physics

The picture before the formula

Most physics anxiety comes from manipulating equations you can't see the meaning of. Ed works the other way round: build the mental picture, then the maths becomes bookkeeping rather than magic.

School level through to first-year undergraduate.

Maths

Foundations to undergraduate

Arithmetic and algebra through to calculus and beyond. Where someone's stuck at a higher level, the cause is usually something much earlier that never quite landed — so that's where Ed goes looking first.

Rebuilding confidence in the basics is often where the real progress hides.

Philosophy

Arguments, analysis, writing

How to read a text closely, spot the move an author is making, and answer the question that was actually asked rather than the one you'd rather answer.

A-level and undergraduate, including essay technique.

How lessons run

What to expect

Online by default

Academic tuition works particularly well online — a shared screen and a digital whiteboard beat a cramped kitchen table. Ed teaches from England or Bilbao depending on the time of year.

Bring your own material

Homework, past papers, a topic you've been dreading, or the specification you're working to. Send it ahead and Ed will have looked at it before you start.

Visual explanations

Diagrams, sketches and analogies rather than walls of algebra. If a picture exists that makes the idea obvious, Ed will find it.

Exams and coursework

Whatever board or syllabus you're on, Ed will work to it — including timed practice and marking against the real criteria.

Lesson length

30, 60, 90 or 120 minutes. Longer sessions suit exam preparation; shorter ones suit weekly upkeep.

Every kind of learner

Ed has taught people of all ages and learning styles, including neurodivergent students. Tell him what helps you and the lesson gets built around that.

The approach

No hierarchy in the room

Ed's line on teaching applies just as much to physics as it does to the violin:

Over the years I've had good teachers and awful, awful teachers. I guarantee we'll have a fun, productive time and get along as equals.
